WTD · Stream control

Go live on Twitch and YouTube, show or hide your Windows screen, set your computer and headset levels, and set up the stream keys for the WithTheDead stream.

1 · Connect to OBS

This OBS WebSocket has no password, so anyone on your network can read the stream key from it, and start or stop the stream. Turn on authentication: in OBS under Tools → WebSocket Server Settings, or on a headless OBS with "auth_required": true and a "server_password" in ~/.config/obs-studio/plugin_config/obs-websocket/config.json (restart OBS after). Then enter the password here and in obs-web.

2 · Go live

not connected

Starts or ends the WithTheDead stream from the OBS on 192.168.1.202, on Twitch and YouTube at once (whichever have a key and are switched on in section 6). It asks first either way, and tells you what viewers will see.

3 · Windows screen

not connected

If sound and picture drift apart (say, after this PC sleeps), this reconnects the feed from this PC. Viewers see the BRB card for about 10 seconds; the stream keeps going. It works through section 4's connection.

Puts your main Windows display, with its sound, on the stream or takes it off. While it's hidden, viewers see the Be-right-back card. Shortcut: S on this page. The picture comes from OBS on this PC, which has to be open and streaming to rtmp://192.168.1.202:1935/live (it starts that way at login).

4 · Audio

Not connected to OBS on this PC.

Your computer's sound and your headset mic are mixed by OBS on this PC (ws://127.0.0.1:4455) before they go to the stream, so their levels are set there. Double-click a slider for 0 dB. Its password is in that OBS under Tools → WebSocket Server Settings → Show Connect Info. Hiding the Windows screen mutes both.

6 · Twitch and YouTube

The relay sends the stream to each one that has a key and is switched on. Switching one on or off mid-stream starts or ends the stream there alone. Anyone with a key can stream to that channel, so never show one on stream.

7 · Twitch bandwidth test (not public)

Streams to Twitch alone for 15 seconds, through the relay, with ?bandwidthtest=true on the key: Twitch takes the video but never shows it, so nobody sees the channel go live, and YouTube gets nothing. It proves the Twitch key, the relay and the connection work, and shows the bitrate that gets through. YouTube has no such test: set the stream to Private in YouTube Studio to try it unseen.
